# Anti-AI Quality Gate

This is a final review layer. Do not erase a strong strategy, verified brand voice, or intentional persuasion merely to sound casual.

## Goal

The output should feel deliberately written for this brand, audience, offer, channel, and moment—not like a reusable generic marketing template.

## Reject generic filler

Remove or rewrite language that adds no concrete meaning, including:

- vague superiority claims with no proof;
- stacked buzzwords;
- motivational filler unrelated to the buying decision;
- repeated statements of the same benefit;
- empty transitions that only make the copy longer;
- generic 'in today's fast-paced world' style openings;
- automatic three-adjective lists used as decoration;
- excessive em dashes, headings, or rhetorical questions used mechanically.

## Protect specificity

Prefer concrete customer situations, observable outcomes, product realities, and natural language over abstract claims.

Do not create fake specificity. A made-up number is worse than a truthful general statement.

## Human rhythm

Vary sentence length naturally. Let short lines exist when they earn emphasis. Do not make every sentence punchy. Do not force every paragraph into the same length or cadence.

Avoid obvious template symmetry such as making every section contain exactly three bullets unless the content naturally supports it.

## Hook discipline

A hook should open a real loop, state a meaningful tension, reveal a useful idea, or frame a relevant customer truth.

Reject hooks that are merely louder versions of the service name.

Do not use clickbait the body cannot satisfy.

## Social and poster copy

A poster is not a newspaper. Keep the visual message hierarchy intentional.

When creative direction is requested:

- define the central visual idea;
- decide what the viewer notices first, second, and third;
- keep on-art copy as short as the concept permits;
- do not paste the caption onto the design;
- do not turn a service list into the visual concept unless listing services is the explicit purpose.

## Persuasion without manipulation

Do not fabricate urgency, scarcity, popularity, testimonials, social proof, deadlines, or fear.

Do not imply guaranteed results unless a verified guarantee exists.

Use psychology to clarify value and reduce friction, not to deceive.

## Brand preservation

Do not 'humanize' by introducing slang or humor that conflicts with brand voice.

Do not remove required terminology simply because it sounds formal.

Preserve intentional regional language when it is part of the brand/audience strategy.

## Final pass

Before delivery ask:

1. Could this copy plausibly belong to ten unrelated brands? If yes, make it more specific.
2. Is there one clear idea, or only a pile of claims?
3. Does the opening earn attention honestly?
4. Is every strong claim supported by known facts?
5. Does the CTA fit the reader's stage?
6. Did the draft become longer than the message needs?
7. Does any phrase sound inserted because 'marketing copy usually says this' rather than because this message needs it?

Revise until the answer passes these checks without losing the strategic intent established by upstream modules.
